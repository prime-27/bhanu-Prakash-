# Bhanu Prakash

Exploring the universe. Making science accessible. Building technology that inspires.

This is the brand of Bhanu Prakash, an astronomer and stargazing expert with an MSc in Astronomy who also designs and builds websites, software and applications. It is built from the portfolio website and its particle universe, and it applies to the site, slides, social posts, workshop handouts, certificates and event posters.

## The idea: from Earth to the universe

Every piece of the brand starts on the ground and looks up. The website is one continuous trip, from a hillside at night to the cosmic web a billion light-years away, and the brand borrows that structure everywhere: a dark sky, one guiding light, and real distances along the way.

Astronomy leads; technology supports. When both appear, astronomy takes the larger space, the first position and the amber accent. Development work uses the cooler `ice` color.

## Content fundamentals

Write the way you would talk to a group at the telescope: short, plain sentences, in the first person for Bhanu and in the second person for the reader.

- **Use real numbers with units.** "The Moon is 384,400 km away, and its light reaches you in 1.3 seconds." Distances, light-travel times, dates and coordinates are the brand's texture.
- **Never invent facts or metrics.** No made-up counts of schools, participants, clients or awards. If a number is not documented, leave it out or mark it as a placeholder.
- **Lead with what people will see or do.** "Look through the telescope yourself," not "Experience a transformative cosmic journey."
- **Name the sky precisely.** Say "Orion Nebula (M42)", not "a beautiful nebula".
- **Calls to action are verbs:** Explore My Work, Get in Touch, Plan a session, Send inquiry.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Sessions run for about two hours, starting after sunset. | Unforgettable magical nights under the stars! |
| Saturn's rings span about 280,000 km. | Saturn's rings are mind-blowingly huge. |
| Telescope basics, lunar craters, eclipses. | Cutting-edge astro-learning solutions. |

## Logo: the Pointer mark

Four stars joined by a dashed sightline lead to one amber guide star, inside an orbit ring. Like the Big Dipper's pointer stars leading to Polaris, the mark is about showing people where to look. Files are in the Logos group.

- **Lockup:** the mark followed by "Bhanu Prakash" in `heading-card` (Space Grotesk 500), with an optional `label` line "Astronomer · Developer" in `dim`. The gap between mark and name is half the mark's width.
- **Clear space:** the ring's radius on every side.
- **Minimum size:** 20px on screen, 8mm in print. Smaller than that, use the guide star alone as an amber dot.
- **Grounds:** the night mark on `void`, `night` or dark photography; the day mark on Day `void` and paper.
- **Don't** recolor the guide star, add glows or gradients, rotate or stretch the mark, or set it on a busy part of a photo.

## Color

Night is the home theme. Day exists for print, PDFs and certificates.

- **Grounds:** `void` for pages, `night` for alternating bands and bars, `panel` for cards and fields.
- **Text:** `star` for headings and key text, `star-2` for long passages, `dim` for labels and captions.
- **One accent:** `amber` is the guide star. Spend it on one or two things per view: the primary action, the eyebrow, the active state. Text placed on amber uses `on-amber`.
- **Supporting hues:** `ice` marks technology and in-progress work; `nebula` is a rare highlight, never a button or link color.
- **Particles** use `particle-warm`, `particle-cool`, `particle-ember` and `particle-gas` together with `star`, `amber`, `nebula` and `ice`, on Night only.
- **Lines:** `line` for every border and rule. The brand separates with hairlines, not shadows.

All text pairs pass 4.5:1 on their grounds in both themes; each token's note gives its ratio.

## Typography

Three Google-hosted families, all free to use:

- **Space Grotesk** (display): headings, chapter titles and data readouts, weight 500 with tight tracking.
- **Instrument Sans** (body): paragraphs, ledes and card text.
- **IBM Plex Mono** (mono): uppercase labels at `label` and measured facts at `coordinate`.

Pair a mono `label` eyebrow in amber above a Space Grotesk heading, then a `lede`. Keep headings to about 20 characters per line and text to about 65. Use tabular figures wherever numbers line up.

## Layout and spacing

Spacing runs on a 4px base with 16px as the working unit: `space-4` gutters, `space-6` card padding, `space-12` before content, `space-26` between sections. Content sits in a 1140px column, text-heavy blocks on the left and the sky on the right. Corners are nearly square (`radius-md`), like instrument panels; only buttons and chips are pills (`radius-pill`).

## The particle universe

The signature visual is a field of tens of thousands of particles that forms the night sky, the Moon, planets, nebulae, a black hole and galaxies, and flies between them as you scroll. The Universe journey section lists the eleven stops.

- Particles only ever form real astronomical objects, built from real structure: Saturn's Cassini gap, Jupiter's Great Red Spot, the Milky Way's spiral arms.
- Motion is driven by the viewer's scroll and pointer, not on loops. Stars twinkle slowly, and the pointer pushes them gently aside.
- Respect reduced motion: show still formations, no streaks or sparks.
- Where nothing can animate (slides, print, social), use the starfield and orbit patterns, or a still frame of a scene.

## Photography

Use real photographs of sessions, telescopes, workshops and the actual night sky, shared with permission and captioned. Dark, natural, lit by red torches and screens. No stock astronauts, rockets, cartoon planets or AI-generated space art.

## Iconography

Hairline icons drawn at 1.5px in `dim` or `star`, built from the same parts as the mark: circles for bodies and rings, dots for stars, dashed lines for sightlines and orbits. No emoji and no filled glossy icons.

## Applications

- **Website:** the reference implementation, Night theme, particle universe behind every section.
- **Slides:** `void` ground, a `label` eyebrow in amber, a `display-chapter` title, the starfield pattern on title slides only.
- **Social posts:** 1080px squares on `void`, one fact with its real number in `readout`, the Pointer mark bottom-left.
- **Certificates and handouts:** Day theme on paper, the day mark, the orbits pattern as a quiet border.
- **Event posters:** a still frame of the target object (for example the Moon or Saturn), the date and venue in `coordinate` style, one amber call to action.
